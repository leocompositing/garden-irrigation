Planting Plan — Oval Border

Curved ring border around the oval lawn · ~18 m² · Coulsdon, Surrey (51°18′N, RHS H5 / USDA 8b) · neutral–slightly alkaline London clay over chalk

🗺 Open the plan-view layout (SVG) →

Context & brief

The curved border wraps the oval lawn (lawn stays as grass). The scheme is purple-led — deep violet, mauve, lilac, plum, with silver-purple and burgundy foliage, supported by white and soft pink. No yellow/orange as feature colours. Target is 60–70% evergreen / 30–40% flowering by planted area in every zone, with staggered colour so no month goes bare. Pets use the garden, so mildly-toxic plants are kept but flagged (see the flag/reject list at the foot). Drainage-sensitive picks (sweet william, coneflower, peony) sit in the best-drained Zone D, raised slightly on horticultural grit.

Climber placement

  • Primary — Zone A warm brick wall (NW, sheltered): Clematis armandii. Evergreen, hardiest of the evergreen climbers, earliest flower (Feb–Mar), copes with the part-shade aspect. This is the one to plant.
  • Optional sun-lover — hottest wall (Zone B/D, SW/S): Trachelospermum jasminoides (star jasmine) if you want summer scent. It needs more heat and sun than Zone A offers and is slightly more tender, so only on the warmest, most sheltered run. Evergreen, white summer flowers.
  • Avoid winter jasmine (Jasminum nudiflorum) — yellow flowers, off-palette.

Flag & reject list

⚠ Pet toxicity — flagged, not removed (your call to keep):
  • Daylily (Hemerocallis) — SEVERELY toxic to cats (kidney failure from tiny amounts, including pollen/water). This is in a different league to the "mild" flags. Strong recommendation: swap for Agapanthus 'Northern Star' or Liriope muscari (see swap list) if a cat uses the garden.
  • Hydrangea (A, C) — mildly toxic (cyanogenic) if leaves/buds chewed.
  • Euphorbia characias (B) — milky sap is a skin/eye irritant and toxic if ingested; wear gloves to prune.
  • Peony (D) — mild GI upset if eaten in quantity.
  • Japanese anemone (A) — sap irritant.
  • Aster, Sweet William, Clematis — mild GI/skin irritants.
  • Pet-safe favourites for reassurance: hardy Geranium, Lavender, Salvia, Bergenia, Heuchera, Rose, Phlox, Weigela, Deutzia, Cornus.
Site / palette rejects & substitutions:
  • Cotoneaster lacteus — dropped from the border. It reaches ~4 m × 4 m and is grown as a hedge/screen; it would swamp an 18 m² ornamental ring and the white-undersided leaf isn't a purple-palette feature. The boundary walls already do the screening job. If you want it, use a single clipped standard as a backdrop accent against the SE wall only — not as a hedge in the bed.
  • Brachyglottis 'Sunshine' — substituted. Its yellow daisies break the no-yellow rule; you'd be shearing flowers off all summer. Replaced with Convolvulus cneorum (silver evergreen, white/pink-budded). Senecio cineraria 'Silver Dust' is the fallback.
  • "Sage" confirmed as ornamental Salvia nemorosa 'Caradonna' (Zone B), not culinary sage.
Soil & drainage note: on London clay over chalk, dig in horticultural grit + compost across the whole border, and especially raise Zone D's planting positions for peony, echinacea and sweet william (add a 30–40% grit mix). Lavender, Cistus, Convolvulus and Euphorbia in Zone B also want grit — they rot in winter wet far sooner than they die of drought. Hydrangea blue/mauve colour depends on acidity: on this alkaline soil it leans pink. Decision: keep the hydrangea and hold the lilac — plant it in an ericaceous-amended pocket and feed annually with a hydrangea colourant (aluminium sulphate) from early spring; rainwater, not tap, in summer. Expect it to drift pinker if the feeding lapses.
